Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an odour assessment to open a restaurant?

In most cases, yes. If you are applying for planning permission to open a restaurant, café, takeaway, dark kitchen or any food business with a commercial kitchen, the local planning authority will almost certainly require an odour assessment as part of your application. This applies whether you are opening a new premises or changing the use of an existing building. The assessment demonstrates that cooking odours will be adequately controlled and will not cause a nuisance to nearby residents or businesses.

What is EMAQ+ and why does it matter?

EMAQ+ is the guidance on controlling odour and noise from commercial kitchen exhaust systems (2018), which updated the earlier Defra guidance. Its risk assessment method scores four factors: dispersion from the discharge, proximity of sensitive receptors, size of kitchen and cooking type. The total gives a risk rating of Low to Medium, High or Very High, and that rating sets the level of odour control the extract system needs to provide. Environmental health officers widely refer to EMAQ+, so using it means your report speaks the same language as the council's officers.

What does a kitchen odour assessment involve?

A kitchen odour assessment reviews the kitchen layout, cooking methods, the extract discharge point and the nearby residential or commercial receptors, from the drawings and details you supply, with a site visit where one is needed. We then apply the EMAQ+ risk assessment method to score the odour risk and state the level of odour control the extract system needs to provide, for your extract specialist to design and install. Where a design already exists, we assess whether it provides that level of control. The output is a clear report ready for submission to the planning authority.
